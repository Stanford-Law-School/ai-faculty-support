# Teaching and student scenarios

Fictional composite patterns for governance in teaching contexts. Label every scenario fictional and non-identifying. Students are affected people, not just users; protect their data, learning, and ability to contest outcomes.

## Scenario patterns

### Source-grounded course assistant
Governance focus: what corpus it reads; whether it can fabricate authority; who owns wrong answers to students; correction path; transparency that it is AI; a non-AI alternative.

### AI-generated formative feedback
Governance focus: feedback is not a grade; human ownership of any consequential judgment; student-data minimization; disclosure; whether review is meaningful at class scale; preserving learning.

### Automated or AI-assisted grading
Governance focus: grading is a human determination; high stakes and low reversibility; equity; contestability; likely institutional review; a legitimate outcome is "do not proceed."

### AI-use concern about a student
Governance focus: the Skill does not determine whether a student used AI; route policy to the Faculty AI Policy Builder; protect the student's ability to respond; no adjudication.

## Cross-cutting requirements

- Provide a genuine non-AI alternative so no student is forced into a tool.
- Do not let AI make or effectively make grading, misconduct, or accommodation decisions.
- Minimize student personal data; prefer none.
- Give students a way to understand and contest AI-influenced outcomes.
