# Evidence and authorization gates

Distinguish what evidence justifies each stage from who may authorize it. Evidence is not authorization, and a successful pilot does not authorize deployment.

## The gates

### Exploration gate
May involve public or synthetic information, individual learning, no consequential action, and no institutional representation.

### Capability gate
Requires a controlled test, documented behavior, known limitations, and safe materials.

### Pilot gate
Requires a defined problem, a baseline, representative test cases, data limits, reviewers, failure rules, stop conditions, and appropriate institutional questions surfaced.

### Continued-use gate
Requires monitoring, incident review, change control, evidence that review remains realistic, and evidence of continuing value.

### Deployment or institutional-use gate
Requires authorization outside this Skill. **The Skill must never grant this authorization.**

## Principles

- Match the gate to the evidence actually available; do not skip gates because a demo was impressive.
- Name the authorized role for each gate (often outside this Skill); mark unknowns as "institutional confirmation required."
- A successful pilot is evidence for a decision, not authorization to deploy.
- Formal pilot protocol design belongs to the Plan an AI Pilot; route there when pilot prerequisites are defined.
