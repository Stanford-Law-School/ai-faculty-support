# Stanford lens

Apply these considerations contextually — only where they affect the use. This is original faculty guidance, not binding Stanford policy, and it is not SLS-specific rules. Do not append a generic ethics checklist that does not change the analysis.

## Mission
- What goal does the system serve?
- Is the proposed access or authority necessary?
- Who benefits? Who bears errors?

## Responsibility
- Who owns the purpose and the result?
- Who reviews, can reject, correct, and stop?
- Who responds to harm?

## Evidence
- What justifies exploration, a pilot, and continued use?
- Which failures require pause?
- Are omissions detectable?

## Data and privacy
- Is each data element necessary?
- Can less or synthetic information be used?
- Who receives the information and accesses logs?
- How is information deleted? Does the purpose change?

## Teaching and research
- What judgment must remain visible?
- Are students, clients, or research participants affected?
- Is an alternative path available? Could the workflow create dependency or deskilling?

## Equity and accessibility
- Can affected people understand or contest the system?
- Are barriers created? Are errors unevenly distributed?
- Is the interface accessible? Is a non-AI alternative available?

## Current-source rule and boundary

When the analysis depends on current Stanford guidance or services, require live research from official sources and record the date (see the web-research reference). Distinguish SLS-specific guidance from Stanford University policy and general guidance. Do not infer authority from an office name, describe University guidance as SLS-specific, or treat absence of a listing as approval or prohibition. This lens grants no authorization and certifies nothing.
