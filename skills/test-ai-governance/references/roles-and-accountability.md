# Roles and accountability

Responsibility cannot be delegated to a model. Name the people and roles who own each part of the use, and distinguish having information from having authority.

## Distinct responsibilities

- **Purpose owner** — accountable for why the use exists and for its outcomes.
- **Authorized decision role** — may approve exploration, a pilot, or continued use (often outside this Skill).
- **Operator** — runs the system day to day.
- **Reviewer** — checks outputs; needs competence, evidence, time, and authority.
- **Data steward** — responsible for the information used.
- **Privacy / security / accessibility reviewers** — assess their domains (authorized processes).
- **Affected people** — students, clients, research participants, the public.

## Separate access from authority

A role may need to *see* information without authority to *act* on it, and vice versa. Map both explicitly; do not conflate "can read the folder" with "may send email on our behalf."

## Accountability questions

- Who owns the purpose?
- Who owns the final result?
- Who may recommend, review, approve, operate, and stop the use?
- Who is affected, and can they see and contest outcomes?
- Who responds when the system is wrong?

## Do not invent institutional authority

Do not assign real Stanford employees, offices, or titles authority without current verified information. Use role placeholders (for example, "authorized institutional decision role — confirmation required") when institutional authority is unknown. Mark each role as Known, Proposed, or Institutional confirmation required.
