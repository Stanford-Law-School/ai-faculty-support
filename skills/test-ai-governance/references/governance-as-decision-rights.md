# Governance as decision rights

Governance is not a document beside a system. It is the allocation, across the whole workflow, of purpose, authority, access, evidence, responsibility, review, correction, escalation, monitoring, and stopping power.

## The connected layers

1. Human purpose — the goal the use serves.
2. Accountable purpose owner — who answers for that purpose.
3. Authorized decision role — who may approve the use.
4. Intended users — who operates it.
5. Affected people — who bears the consequences.
6. Data and source materials — what it works from.
7. Access permissions — what it may reach.
8. System actions — what it may do.
9. Human-confirmation points — where a person must approve before an action.
10. Review and verification — who checks outputs, how.
11. Evidence gates — what justifies each stage.
12. Authorization gates — who may permit each stage.
13. Transparency and notice — what users and affected people are told.
14. Correction and contestability — how errors are reported and fixed.
15. Monitoring and logging — what is observed and recorded.
16. Escalation and incident response — what happens when things go wrong.
17. Stop authority — who can halt the use.
18. Change control — what triggers renewed review.
19. Retirement, deletion, and exit — how the use ends.

## Gaps live between layers

Common failures are not missing controls but disconnected ones:

- Strong technical controls but no owner for incorrect public answers.
- A faculty reviewer assigned without enough time to detect omissions.
- An agent that requires confirmation before sending but has excessive read access.
- Good logs with no person responsible for reviewing them.
- A pilot that "passed" with no authorized deployment decision.

## Proportionality

Match governance effort to consequence. A low-stakes, public, reversible use needs light governance; a consequential, irreversible, or sensitive use needs strong ownership, review, and stopping power. More oversight is not always better — it must be the right oversight at the right layer.
