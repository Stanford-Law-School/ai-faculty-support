# Human judgment framework

Meaningful human judgment is a set of concrete conditions, not a phrase. A person is only meaningfully "in the loop" when they can actually detect and correct the failure that matters.

## Conditions for meaningful judgment

- Defined responsibility
- Relevant competence
- Access to the evidence needed to judge
- Adequate time
- Authority to reject
- Authority to correct
- Authority to escalate
- Authority to stop
- Independence
- Traceability
- Accountability

## Judgments that must remain human

Consequential determinations stay with accountable people: grading, misconduct, admissions, employment, accommodations, and whether a student used AI. AI may inform or prepare; it does not own these.

## Do not accept

- "A professor will review everything" as a sufficient oversight plan.
- A reviewer described as in the loop who lacks the time, evidence, competence, or authority to catch and fix the relevant failure — that is ceremonial oversight.
- Review of fluent output without the source needed to detect a fabricated citation or a silent omission.
