# Correction, appeal, and redress

Governance is incomplete without a way to catch, fix, and learn from wrong outcomes. Design correction before the system is used, not after the first incident.

## Correction and redress steps

For consequential or public-facing use, define:

1. How an error is reported
2. Who receives the report
3. Who preserves relevant evidence
4. Who checks the source
5. Who checks the system behavior
6. How immediate harm is limited
7. How the output or record is corrected
8. Whether downstream recipients must be notified
9. Whether similar outputs must be checked
10. Whether the workflow pauses
11. How the affected person may contest the response
12. How recurrence is prevented
13. What is logged
14. Who decides whether use resumes

## Principles

- The reporter should reach a human with authority to act, not a dead end.
- Preserve evidence before "fixing" so the cause can be understood.
- A single confirmed error often implies checking similar outputs, not just the one reported.
- Redress includes correcting downstream effects, not only the original output.

## Boundary

Do not promise a specific Stanford procedure unless current official guidance establishes it; where institutional process is unknown, mark it "institutional confirmation required" and design a reasonable interim path.
