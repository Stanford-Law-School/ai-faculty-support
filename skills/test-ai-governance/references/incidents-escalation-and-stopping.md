# Incidents, escalation, and stopping

Define, in advance, how problems are classified, who is notified, and who can halt the use. The absence of a practical stop mechanism is itself a governance gap.

## Incident tiers

### Routine correction
A reversible, limited problem handled through normal review.

### Material incident
A consequential error, misleading omission, access problem, repeated failure, or correction failure requiring escalation.

### Critical incident
Examples: restricted-data exposure; unauthorized external action; fabricated authority used consequentially; incorrect high-stakes determination; permission escalation; untraceable system action; significant accessibility exclusion; repeated critical failure after mitigation.

## Stop authority

Every substantial governance package states:

- Who may pause one user's activity
- Who may pause a pilot
- Who may disable a connector
- Who may revoke permissions
- Who may stop external action
- Who decides whether use resumes
- What evidence is required before resumption

Stop authority must be a real person or role, reachable, and empowered to act without waiting for the tool's or vendor's agreement.

## Boundary

Do not invent institutional incident-reporting obligations; use visible placeholders for institutional confirmation. Treat "no one can actually stop this quickly" as a blocking gap for consequential uses.
