# Vendors, contracts, and control gaps

A vendor assurance is not an institutional decision, and a contract clause is not a technical control. Separate the kinds of "guarantee" so faculty see what is actually settled.

## Distinguish the layers

- Public product statement (marketing)
- Contractual promise (what the vendor agreed)
- Technical configuration (what the system actually does)
- Administrative control (institutional settings)
- User practice (what people actually do)
- Institutional policy (what is authorized)
- Independent evidence (verified behavior)

A claim at one layer does not establish the others.

## "No training on customer data" does not answer

- Retention
- Logs
- Human access
- Product improvement
- Subprocessors
- Deletion
- Account-tier differences
- Data classification
- Institutional authorization

## Principles

- Vendor participation in a pilot is normal but must not settle governance; preserve independent judgment.
- Treat vendor claims as claims to verify, not as independent evidence.
- Identify which questions require authorized legal, procurement, privacy, security, accessibility, or institutional review — and route them.

## Boundary

Do not perform contract legal review or certify compliance. Name the review each open question needs; if it depends on current product terms or Stanford guidance, require live research (see the web-research reference) and route architecture/terms questions to the Understand an AI Tool.
