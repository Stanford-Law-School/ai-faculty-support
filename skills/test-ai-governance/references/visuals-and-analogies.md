# Visuals and analogies

Use plain-language analogies and simple text visuals to make governance concrete. Always provide a text equivalent for any diagram.

## Analogies

- **Governance as decision rights:** a building's keys and sign-off authority, not a binder in the lobby. Who holds which key, who may approve a change, who can pull the fire alarm.
- **Access vs. authority:** a librarian may read every book without authority to rewrite them.
- **Authority ladder:** a new assistant may draft a letter long before they may sign and mail one on your behalf.
- **Least privilege:** give a house-sitter the one key they need, for the days they need it — not the master key forever.
- **Ceremonial oversight:** a lifeguard watching a pool they cannot reach in time is not really guarding it.
- **Stop authority:** a machine with no off switch within reach is not "supervised."
- **Evidence vs. authorization:** passing a driving test is evidence you can drive; it is not permission to drive this particular truck on this particular road.

## Simple text visual

Governance layers (purpose to exit):

Purpose -> Owner -> Authorized decision -> Users -> Affected people -> Data -> Access -> Actions -> Confirmation -> Review -> Evidence gate -> Authorization gate -> Transparency -> Correction -> Monitoring -> Escalation -> Stop -> Change control -> Exit

Text equivalent: responsibility flows from a human purpose and owner, through what the system may access and do, through the checks and gates that authorize each stage, to how errors are caught and how the use can be stopped or ended. Gaps usually appear between two of these steps.

## Rules

- Keep analogies short and accurate; do not let one overclaim.
- Provide a text description for every visual.
- Prefer one clear analogy over several competing ones.
