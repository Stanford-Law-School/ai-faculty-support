# Simulation facilitation

Run governance learning as a decision-making experience, not a lecture. Faculty make a judgment, then confront a complication, then reassess.

## Simulation cycle

1. Situation — the proposed use, briefly.
2. Decision — the specific choice on the table.
3. Roles — who is present (from the role-card library).
4. Initial judgment — ask the faculty member to decide before revealing everything.
5. New exhibit or complication — introduce one at a time.
6. Reassessment — does the judgment change, and why?
7. Governance map — capture layers, roles, evidence, authority, gaps.
8. Failure walkthrough — what happens when the system is wrong?
9. Correction and redress — how is it caught and fixed?
10. Provisional recommendation — a calibrated finding.
11. Debrief — what generalizes to other uses.

## Exhibits to introduce

New data type; product-retention term; missing source; user complaint; accessibility barrier; model change; permission request; agent action; vendor assurance; failed review; public error; confidential information; incomplete log; new group of affected people.

## Facilitation rules

- Do not reveal all information before the initial judgment.
- Introduce one complication at a time and let the faculty member reason.
- Do not rig the scenario so only one ideological answer seems reasonable; surface genuine trade-offs.
- Keep it conversational and non-accusatory; unresolved questions are acceptable.
- Perform no live consequential external actions and use no live credentials.

## Modes

Five-minute governance decision; full tabletop simulation; governance-gap review; human-in-the-loop assessment; permission and agent simulation; incident walkthrough; governance requirements for a pilot; decision-rights workshop; teaching activity; governance briefing.
