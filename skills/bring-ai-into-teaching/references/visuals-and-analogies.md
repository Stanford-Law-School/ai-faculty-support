# Visuals and analogies

Use plain-language analogies and simple text visuals to help faculty reason about teaching design. Always provide a text equivalent for any diagram.

## Analogies

- **Objective before tool:** you choose the exercise for the muscle you want to build, then decide whether a machine helps — not the reverse.
- **Foundational work first:** a calculator helps an accountant and stunts a student still learning arithmetic; the same tool, different learning stage.
- **Critique over generation:** learning to spot a flawed brief builds more judgment than receiving a polished one.
- **Product vs literacy:** knowing where the buttons are is not knowing why the output can be wrong.
- **Output vs learning:** a beautiful essay a student cannot explain is a portrait, not a skill.
- **No-tool alternative:** a ramp beside the stairs — same destination, no one excluded.

## Simple text visual

Design path:

Learning objective -> Foundational (no-AI) work -> Posture choice -> Visible reasoning -> Verify sources -> No-tool alternative + accessibility -> Debrief -> Evidence of learning -> Faculty test

Text equivalent: start from the objective and the work students must do themselves; choose the lightest AI posture that fits; keep reasoning visible and sources verified; guarantee an equivalent, accessible non-AI path; debrief; collect evidence of learning; and test the activity before assigning it.

## Rules

- Keep analogies short and accurate; do not let one overclaim.
- Provide a text description for every visual.
- Prefer one clear analogy over several competing ones.
