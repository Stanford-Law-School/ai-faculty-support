# Legal research and source integrity

In legal-research activities, authoritative research establishes the source base before AI is used to summarize, compare, or synthesize. Generated legal language is not research.

## Require

- Traditional and authoritative research first.
- Verification that each source exists.
- Verification that the source supports the proposition it is cited for.
- Correct jurisdiction.
- Correct date and current status when required.
- Binding versus persuasive authority.
- Contrary authority.
- Search and verification records.
- A clear line between AI synthesis and authoritative research.

## Activity patterns

Citation audit; authority ladder (binding to persuasive); missing-authority exercise; traditional-first comparison; search-strategy comparison; source-grounded revision; procedural-posture check; rule-with-exception exercise.

## Hard rules

- Do not describe generated legal language as authoritative research.
- Do not claim citator-level verification (good law / negative treatment) without an actual citator; a language model does not perform citator checks.
- Fictional legal materials used as teaching inputs must be labeled: **FICTIONAL INSTRUCTIONAL MATERIAL — not real legal authority.**
- Real authorities used in an activity must be verified by the faculty member.

## Handoff

Statements of current substantive law route to authoritative legal research; verified readings to the Reading List and Course Materials Curator.
