# Oral advocacy and discussion activities

Use AI to stress-test arguments and generate practice prompts, while the student's own preparation and judgment remain primary.

## Patterns

- AI-generated bench questions students must answer, then evaluate for relevance and difficulty.
- Argument stress testing: AI raises objections; students respond and judge which objections are sound.
- Counterargument generation students must accept, refute, or reframe.
- Oral-response critique: students critique an AI-suggested answer against professional standards.
- Negotiation scenarios (fictional composite) with AI as a counterpart or coach.
- Socratic-question analysis: students evaluate whether AI-suggested questions probe the right reasoning.

## Require students to

- Prepare an independent position first.
- Evaluate AI suggestions rather than adopt them.
- Defend their choices.
- Revise based on evidence, not on the AI's confidence.
- Identify irrelevant, wrong, or inappropriate suggestions.

## Rules

- Do not use real confidential client information; use fictional composite scenarios.
- Keep the student's own advocacy and judgment as the assessed skill.
- Provide a no-tool path (peer or faculty questioner) so participation does not depend on tool access.

## Handoff

For structured question sequences and hypotheticals, route to the Socratic Question and Hypothetical Designer.
