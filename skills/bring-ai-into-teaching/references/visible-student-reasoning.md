# Visible student reasoning

Design activities so faculty can see the student's thinking, not just a finished product. Request concise rationale and evidence — never hidden chain-of-thought.

## What should remain visible

That students can identify issues, select and distinguish authority, apply rules, evaluate evidence, recognize uncertainty, explain tradeoffs, exercise professional judgment, verify a source, revise an argument, defend a decision, and identify limitations.

## Methods

Pre-AI prediction; independent initial analysis; source log; prompt and output record; verification table; error annotation; comparison memo; revision explanation; oral defense; decision journal; citation audit; claim-evidence matrix; process appendix.

## What to request (and not)

- Request concise rationale, decisions, evidence, verification steps, and revision explanations.
- Do NOT request hidden chain-of-thought or a model's internal reasoning; ask the student to show their own reasoning and choices.
- Prefer artifacts that reveal judgment (why this authority, why this revision) over artifacts that only show output.

## Why it matters

Visible reasoning is how output quality is separated from learning. A polished AI-assisted product with no visible student reasoning is not evidence of learning; a rough product with a clear, well-verified rationale often is.
