# Assessment and learning evidence

Distinguish a good product from evidence that the student learned. A polished AI-assisted deliverable is not proof of learning.

## Evidence of output quality

The product is polished, organized, accurate, or efficient. This says something about the tool and the workflow, not necessarily about the student.

## Evidence of student learning

The student can explain, verify, apply, distinguish, revise, defend, recognize uncertainty, identify error, exercise judgment, and transfer the lesson.

## Possible learning evidence

Pre/post explanation; verification log; error analysis; source audit; revision memo; oral defense; reflection; comparison table; decision rationale; a new hypothetical the student writes; process documentation.

## Do not infer learning from

Tool use; satisfaction; speed; prompt length; number of outputs; a polished final document.

## Design rule

Build at least one artifact that reveals the student's reasoning or verification, and assess that — not the fluency of the AI-assisted output. Where a grade is involved, hand the rubric design to the Assessment and Rubric Builder.

## Handoff

Route complete grading and rubric design to the SLS Assessment and Rubric Builder; this reference identifies what would count as evidence, not the graded instrument.
