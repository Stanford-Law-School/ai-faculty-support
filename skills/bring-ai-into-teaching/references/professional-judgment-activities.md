# Professional-judgment activities

Design activities that develop the professional responsibility and judgment lawyers must exercise around AI — using fictional composite scenarios.

## Themes

Confidentiality; competence; supervision; client communication; candor; source verification; delegation; bias; responsibility; explainability; recordkeeping; disclosure; professional identity; procurement; institutional governance.

## Students should identify

- Who is responsible.
- What evidence is required.
- What may be delegated.
- What must remain human.
- What should be disclosed.
- What must be verified.
- When use should stop.
- Who may be affected.

## Patterns

- A supervising-attorney scenario: an associate used AI for a filing; students identify verification, supervision, and candor duties.
- A confidentiality scenario: what may or may not be entered into a tool, and why.
- A competence scenario: what a lawyer must understand before relying on an AI output.
- A disclosure scenario: when and how AI use should be disclosed to a client or court.

## Limits

- Do not give definitive professional-responsibility conclusions without current authoritative research; frame duties as issues to analyze and verify.
- Use fictional composite scenarios; label them; do not use real client information.
- The activity develops judgment; it does not adjudicate any real person's conduct.
