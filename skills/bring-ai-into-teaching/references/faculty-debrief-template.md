# Faculty debrief template

A facilitator's guide for running the debrief and capturing evidence of learning.

## Debrief Guide

- **Learning objective (restate):**
- **Time allotted:**
- **Format:** pair discussion / short memo / poll / exit ticket / error annotation / oral defense / faculty-led discussion
- **Core questions:**
  - What did you predict, and what changed your judgment?
  - What did the system omit, and what evidence did it actually use?
  - What did it appear to know but could not establish?
  - When did it help, and when did it create more work?
  - What remained your responsibility?
  - What does this example NOT prove?
- **Connect to professional practice:** (how this transfers to real legal work)
- **Evidence of learning to collect:** (revised mental model, error annotation, verification log, decision rationale)
- **Common misconceptions to surface and correct:**
- **Follow-up or next activity:**

## Facilitation notes

- Keep the debrief inside the activity's time budget.
- Draw out reasoning, not just conclusions; ask "how do you know?"
- End on the generalization limit ("what does this not prove?") so students do not over- or under-trust AI from one example.

## Purpose

The debrief is where the activity's learning is consolidated and made visible; treat it as assessed evidence, not an afterthought.
