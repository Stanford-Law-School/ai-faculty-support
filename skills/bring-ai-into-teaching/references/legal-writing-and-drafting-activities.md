# Legal-writing and drafting activities

Keep the legal analysis with the student; use AI, if at all, on parts that are not the assessed skill. Polished prose is not legal analysis.

## Skills to target

Issue statements; rule synthesis; argument organization; counterargument; editing; audience adaptation; professional tone; citation support; revision; process reflection.

## Distinguish the sub-tasks

Brainstorming; outlining; drafting; rewriting; editing; source checking; final authorship judgment. Decide for each whether AI is absent, a critique object, or a constrained tool — based on which sub-task is the learning objective.

## Patterns

- Students draft the analysis independently; AI is used only to suggest edits to organization or tone, and students explain which suggestions they accepted and why.
- Students critique an AI-drafted argument for unsupported assertions and missing counterarguments.
- Students revise an AI draft to add pin cites to real, verified authority.
- Students compare their issue statement with an AI issue statement and defend the differences.

## Rules

- Require students to explain what they changed and why (revision memo).
- Do not let polished prose substitute for legal analysis or for evidence of learning.
- If the objective is the student's own drafting, keep AI out of the drafting step and use it only for critique afterward.
