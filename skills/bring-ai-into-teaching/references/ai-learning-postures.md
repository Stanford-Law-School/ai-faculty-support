# AI learning postures

Choose the posture that fits the objective. These are options, not a ladder — more AI is not better, and "AI absent by design" is a first-class choice.

## AI absent by design
Use when independent practice is the objective, AI would conceal the assessed skill, the material is inappropriate for the tool, access cannot be equitable, or a conventional activity is stronger.

## AI awareness
Students learn concepts without using an AI product (system-layer diagram, misconception analysis, governance scenario, product-independent terminology).

## Faculty demonstration
Faculty demonstrate a behavior or output while students observe and analyze. Use when access is uneven, student tool operation is not the objective, or a controlled demonstration suffices.

## AI as an object of critique
Students examine supplied AI output: verify citations, identify omissions, diagnose jurisdiction errors, compare confidence with evidence, identify unsupported claims.

## Constrained student use
Students use a permitted tool for a defined task. Require a clear boundary, data restrictions, disclosure, source verification, visible reasoning, a debrief, and an access/alternative plan.

## AI as a comparison partner
Students compare AI with human approaches, with authoritative sources, two outputs, grounded vs ungrounded output, or AI recommendations against professional standards.

## AI-infused workflow
Only when the workflow itself is a learning objective and human responsibility is explicit, students document their process, verification and reflection are assessed, access is equitable, and data is appropriate.

## AI governance simulation
Students decide access, permissions, human judgment, evidence, notice, correction, escalation, and stopping (hand deeper work to the Test AI Governance).

## AI-free alternative
A parallel path with equivalent learning value, effort, support, and feedback opportunity.

## Choosing

Match the posture to the objective and to what must stay human and visible. When in doubt, prefer the lightest posture that still meets the objective, and pair any student use with independent foundational work and a debrief.
