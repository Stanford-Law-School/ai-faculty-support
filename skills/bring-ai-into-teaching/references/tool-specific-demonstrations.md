# Tool-specific demonstrations

When a named current product or feature genuinely matters to the activity, research it live and keep the core lesson product-independent.

## When a named product matters

- Require live web research.
- Identify the product, the feature, and the tier.
- Record the date checked.
- Check current official product documentation.
- Check current Stanford public information when relevant.
- Distinguish documentation from institutional approval.
- Provide an alternative when access is unavailable.
- Explain which parts of the lesson remain product-independent.
- State that features may change.

## Keep the lesson durable

Anchor the activity in a durable concept (grounding, omission, verification) and treat the specific product as one current illustration. If the product changes, the concept and most of the activity survive.

## Access and fairness

If the demonstration depends on a paid tier or an account not all students have, prefer a faculty demonstration or supplied output so no student is excluded, and note the access limitation.

## Boundary

Do not perform complete product due diligence here — route that to the Understand an AI Tool. Do not claim a product is Stanford-approved without current authoritative evidence.
