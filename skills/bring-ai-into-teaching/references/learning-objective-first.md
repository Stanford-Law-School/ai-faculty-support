# Learning objective first

Start every design from what students should understand or be able to do, and the legal or professional judgment being developed — never from a tool.

## Define the objective

- What should students understand or be able to do after the activity?
- Which legal, disciplinary, or professional judgment is being developed?
- How will you know the objective was met (evidence, not vibes)?

State it without naming a tool. If the objective can only be stated as "use ChatGPT," it is not yet a learning objective.

## Then ask whether AI helps

Only after the objective is clear, ask: would AI make the target understanding or judgment more visible, testable, or discussable — or would it conceal the very skill being taught?

Common answers:

- AI conceals the skill → keep it out (or use critique/comparison after independent work).
- AI makes a failure mode visible → critique or comparison posture.
- AI is the object of study (literacy) → awareness or demonstration posture.
- The workflow itself is the objective → constrained use or AI-infused workflow with safeguards.

## Guardrails

- AI does not belong in every activity; "AI not needed" is a legitimate, common result.
- Do not add AI because it is current, or to signal innovation.
- Do not remove AI to signal rigor; decide on the objective's merits.
- Product fluency is not a learning objective unless the course specifically requires product operation.

Carry the objective into the Teaching Transfer Map and the posture decision.
