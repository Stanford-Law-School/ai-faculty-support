# Doctrinal and case-analysis activities

Use AI to sharpen — not replace — doctrinal reasoning. Students do the analysis first; AI output becomes something to test against.

## Skills to target

Rule identification; fact sensitivity; case comparison; procedural posture; majority and dissent; counterargument; policy rationale; application to hypotheticals; jurisdictional variation; historical development.

## Activity examples

- Students brief a case before seeing an AI brief, then identify what the AI missed or oversimplified.
- Students identify a material omission in an AI case summary against the assigned opinion.
- Students change one fact and compare how the legal reasoning should change.
- Students determine whether cited language is holding, dicta, or commentary.
- Students compare AI analysis with the assigned authority and mark divergences.
- Students revise an overbroad AI rule statement to match the case.

## Rules

- Verify real authorities used in the activity.
- Label any fictional case, statute, or authority: **FICTIONAL INSTRUCTIONAL MATERIAL — not real legal authority.**
- Require students to cite the passage supporting each claim, so "the AI said so" is never sufficient.
- Keep the independent brief or analysis as the primary evidence of learning; the AI comparison is secondary.
