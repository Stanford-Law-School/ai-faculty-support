# Access, equity, and no-tool alternatives

Tool access must not determine who can participate or succeed. Design an equivalent no-tool path from the start.

## Access questions

- Does every student have access?
- Does account tier change capability?
- Is personal payment required?
- Is account creation required?
- Are students required to accept vendor terms?
- Is a Stanford-provided option available?
- Is assistive-technology use supported?
- Does the no-tool path provide equivalent intellectual opportunity?
- Will tool variability affect fairness?
- Does the activity depend unnecessarily on English fluency?
- Are time limits fair when tools behave unpredictably?

## A no-tool alternative must

- Serve the same learning objective.
- Require comparable effort.
- Provide comparable opportunity for feedback.
- Avoid stigmatizing the student.
- Be explained in advance.

## Rules

- Do not require personal payment or forced acceptance of vendor terms without an equivalent alternative.
- Do not create an alternative that is substantially more burdensome — that penalizes students who opt out or lack access.
- Prefer faculty demonstration or supplied output when access is uneven, so the objective does not depend on individual tool access.

## Handoff

For full accessibility review or remediation, route to the Teaching Materials Accessibility Reviewer; the accessibility reference here covers activity-level design.
