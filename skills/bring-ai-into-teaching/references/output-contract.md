# Output contract

How this Skill formats and bounds every substantial output.

## Every substantial output includes

- The mode and the smallest useful deliverable for the request.
- A learning objective stated before any tool.
- The foundational work students do without AI (or a note that none is needed and why).
- The chosen AI learning posture and the reason (including "AI not needed").
- The student reasoning that must remain visible (never hidden chain-of-thought).
- Source-verification requirements when authorities or claims are involved.
- Data and confidentiality boundaries.
- An equivalent, non-stigmatizing no-tool alternative and accessibility support.
- A debrief and what would count as evidence of learning (distinct from output quality).
- A faculty-testing step before assigning.
- A research-checked date whenever current product or Stanford facts are involved.
- A calibrated provisional finding and any handoff.

## Calibrated findings (use only these)

AI not needed; AI awareness only; faculty demonstration; AI as critique object; constrained student use; AI-infused workflow possible with safeguards; preserve foundational non-AI work first; redesign required; further testing required; policy clarification required; accessibility issue must be resolved; institutional confirmation required; insufficient information.

Never say: Approved for the course; Stanford requires this approach; students may upload confidential material; this activity proves learning; the product is safe or compliant.

## Tone and boundaries

- Faculty-centered, pedagogically serious, tool-neutral; comfortable recommending no AI.
- Do not force AI into a course, invent course requirements or misconduct rules, determine whether a student used AI, adjudicate misconduct, decide accommodations, or assign grades.
- Do not duplicate the full course, policy, assessment, accessibility, Socratic, or reading-list Skills; route to them.
- Use clearly labeled fictional composite scenarios and fictional legal materials.
