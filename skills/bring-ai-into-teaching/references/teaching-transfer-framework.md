# Teaching-transfer framework

The framework turns AI understanding into a defensible teaching decision. Work the sequence; capture it in the Teaching Transfer Map.

## Sequence

1. Course or learning context.
2. Students and prior knowledge.
3. Learning objective.
4. Legal, disciplinary, or professional judgment involved.
5. Foundational work students should do without AI first.
6. Reasoning that must remain visible.
7. Appropriate AI learning posture.
8. Evidence students must inspect.
9. Source-verification requirements.
10. Data and confidentiality boundaries.
11. Meaningful no-tool alternative.
12. Accessibility and participation support.
13. Clear student instructions.
14. Debrief.
15. Evidence of learning.
16. Faculty testing before use.
17. Policy, assessment, accessibility, or institutional handoffs.
18. Provisional recommendation.

## Principles carried through the sequence

- Objective before tool; AI is optional, not assumed.
- Foundational skill work stays human when the activity is meant to build it.
- Student reasoning stays visible; polished output is not proof of learning.
- Sources are verified; authoritative legal research precedes AI synthesis.
- Access and accessibility are designed in, with an equivalent no-tool path.
- Every substantive activity is debriefed and tested before assignment.

## Output

Produce the smallest useful deliverable (an AI Inclusion Decision or a Five-Minute Teaching Moment) and expand to a full activity plan, handout, debrief, and learning-evidence plan only as the faculty member needs. End with a calibrated provisional recommendation and any handoff.
