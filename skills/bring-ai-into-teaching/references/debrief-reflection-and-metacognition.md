# Debrief, reflection, and metacognition

The debrief is part of the activity, not an optional add-on. It is where an AI interaction becomes learning.

## Debrief questions

- What did you predict?
- What changed your judgment?
- Which part was easiest to verify?
- Which failure was hardest to notice?
- What did the system omit?
- What evidence did it use?
- What did it appear to know but could not establish?
- When did it help?
- When did it create additional work?
- What remained your responsibility?
- What would you do differently in professional practice?
- What does this example not prove?

## Formats

Pair discussion; short memo; poll; exit ticket; error annotation; revised mental model; oral defense; decision memo; verification checklist; faculty-led discussion.

## Design rules

- Build the debrief into the activity's time, not after it.
- Tie the debrief back to the learning objective and to professional judgment.
- Include the "what does this not prove?" question so students generalize responsibly.
- Prefer debrief artifacts that also serve as evidence of learning (a revised mental model, an error annotation).

## Purpose

The debrief converts a single AI experience into transferable understanding — what to trust, what to verify, and what remains the lawyer's responsibility.
