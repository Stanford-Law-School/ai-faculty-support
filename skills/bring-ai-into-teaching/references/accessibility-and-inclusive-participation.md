# Accessibility and inclusive participation

Design accessibility into the activity rather than retrofitting it. Route full remediation to the Teaching Materials Accessibility Reviewer.

## Review

Heading structure; plain-language directions; keyboard access; screen-reader use; captioning; transcripts; color-independent meaning; accessible files; reading order; cognitive load; timed interaction; rapid-response expectations; group-work structure; multiple participation paths; sensory content; motion; text alternatives.

## Activity-design implications

- Provide instructions and materials in accessible formats and structure.
- Offer more than one way to participate (written, oral, paired) so one channel is not required.
- Avoid tight timers when tool behavior is unpredictable; timing pressure compounds access barriers.
- Ensure any AI interface used has a keyboard- and screen-reader-usable alternative, or use a faculty demonstration instead.
- Provide text alternatives for any visual or audio AI output students must analyze.

## Rules

- Do not claim formal accessibility compliance (a specific standard) without qualified review.
- Treat a significant accessibility barrier as something to resolve before assigning the activity, not a footnote.
- The no-tool alternative must itself be accessible.

## Handoff

Route full accessibility review or document remediation to the Teaching Materials Accessibility Reviewer.
