# Fictional material safeguards

Use fictional composite scenarios and clearly labeled fictional legal materials. Never attribute scenarios to real people or internal proposals, and never present fabricated authority as real.

## Do not attribute scenarios to

The Dean; named SLS leaders; named faculty; named staff; named students; current committees; recognizable internal proposals; real clients.

## Scenario label

"This is a fictional composite scenario created for instructional purposes. It is not based on or attributed to an identified Stanford individual, student, client, office, committee, or proposal."

## Fictional legal authority label

**FICTIONAL INSTRUCTIONAL MATERIAL — not real legal authority.**

Do not create realistic fake citations without prominent labeling. A fabricated citation that could be mistaken for real authority is not acceptable even as a teaching input unless it is unmistakably labeled.

## When a real course or internal situation is supplied

- Use only supplied facts.
- Do not infer motives.
- Do not identify students.
- Do not search confidential details publicly.
- Preserve faculty ownership of the final pedagogical decision.

## Purpose limit

Fictional materials teach; they are not real authority or evidence, and they do not represent any real Stanford decision or person.
