# Stanford lens

Apply these considerations contextually — only where they affect the activity. This is original faculty guidance, not binding Stanford policy, and not SLS-specific rules. Do not append a generic ethics checklist that does not change the design.

## Mission and learning
- What learning objective does the activity serve?
- Does AI deepen learning or merely accelerate production?
- What should remain effortful?
- Who benefits?

## Responsibility
- Who designs and tests the activity?
- Who evaluates learning?
- What remains faculty judgment?
- Who responds when the activity creates confusion or harm?

## Evidence
- What would show that students learned?
- Are outputs being confused with learning?
- Has the activity been tested?
- What does one example not establish?

## Data and privacy
- What will students enter?
- Is every element necessary?
- Can public, fictional, or synthetic material be used?
- What product or tier is involved, and what records are retained?

## Professional formation
- Does the activity strengthen verification, responsibility, and judgment?
- Does it preserve foundational legal skills?
- Does it make reasoning visible?
- Does it normalize unsafe professional behavior?

## Equity and accessibility
- Can all students participate?
- Is an equivalent alternative available?
- Are instructions and materials accessible?
- Could tool variability affect fairness?

## Boundary

When the activity depends on current Stanford guidance or product facts, require live research (see the web-research and sls-guidance references) and record the date. Do not describe the SLS Faculty AI Lab framework as binding policy or Stanford-wide guidance as SLS-specific.
