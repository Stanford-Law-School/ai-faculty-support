# Clinical and sensitive contexts

When an activity touches real people or protected information, stop before recommending operational tool use and teach through safe simulation instead.

## Trigger contexts

Clients; clinics; privileged information; confidential facts; student records; unpublished interviews; human-subject research; identifiable individuals; trauma; immigration status; medical information; employment matters; criminal victimization.

## Required posture

- Stop before recommending operational use of a tool on the real material.
- Use fictional or synthetic substitutes that preserve the learning objective.
- Identify the institutional or professional questions that must be resolved (privilege, confidentiality, IRB/human-subjects, professional responsibility) and route them.
- Do not enter real sensitive material into any tool.
- Include trauma-informed and accessibility considerations when relevant.

## Preserve the objective safely

The learning objective (interviewing skill, case analysis, client counseling judgment) can almost always be taught with a clearly labeled fictional composite. Design that substitute rather than compromising real confidentiality.

## Boundary

Do not authorize use of protected material, certify compliance, or perform privacy/ethics review. Name the authorized review needed. For clinics with real clients, defer to clinic supervision and professional-responsibility rules.
