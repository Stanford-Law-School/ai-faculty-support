# Risk, reversibility, and verifiability

Three questions shape how cautious a use must be: how bad is an error, can it be undone, and can a human catch it before it matters.

## Stakes

- Who is affected by an error, and how badly?
- Does the output influence a grade, an admission, a client, a publication, or a public statement?
- Higher stakes demand stronger verification and more human ownership.

## Reversibility

- Can the action be undone if wrong?
- Reversible, low-cost-to-undo uses (a draft the author revises) are safer than irreversible ones (an email already sent, a record already changed, a grade already posted).
- Irreversible or consequential actions should not be automated without strong oversight.

## Verifiability

- Can a human check the output against a source or standard?
- How long does verification take, and is the reviewer expert enough?
- Watch for outputs that are hard to verify because errors are plausible (fabricated citations, silent omissions).

## Combined reading

| Stakes | Reversibility | Verifiability | Posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Easy | Easy | Light-touch; low-risk use may be fine |
| High | Hard | Hard | Strong caution; likely keep human or require review |
| High | Easy | Easy | Possible with defined human review |
| Any | Any | Hard | Add verification design or reconsider |

The point is not to forbid risk but to size the safeguards to it, and to say plainly when the safeguards cost more than the benefit.
