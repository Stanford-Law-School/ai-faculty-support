# Current-state workflow map

Map how the work is actually done now, before proposing any change. You cannot judge whether AI helps a process you have not described.

## Map the steps

For each step, capture:

| Step | Who does it | Input | Output | Time/effort | Where it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Walk the whole path from trigger to completion, including handoffs, waiting, and rework.

## Establish a baseline

Record what is true now, where known:

- Volume (how many, how often)
- Time per instance and total
- Quality and error rate
- Omissions
- Who is burdened
- Current cost and access
- How failures are currently handled

If numbers are unknown, say so and estimate a small sample rather than inventing figures.

## Find the real bottleneck

- Which step consumes the most time or attention?
- Which step causes the most rework or escalation?
- Is the bottleneck actually where the frustration is felt, or upstream of it?

## Note

- Include the "do nothing" cost — what continuing as-is actually costs.
- Distinguish steps that add value from steps that exist only out of habit; the latter may be removed rather than automated.

Carry the bottleneck and baseline into root-cause analysis and task decomposition.
