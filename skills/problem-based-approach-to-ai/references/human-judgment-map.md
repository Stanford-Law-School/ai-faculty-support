# Human-judgment map

"A human remains in the loop" is not a safeguard until you say exactly which human, doing exactly what, with what authority. Map it concretely.

## For each retained human role, specify

- The human role (who, by role not name)
- The decision they own
- The output they review
- The evidence they need to review it well
- The expertise required
- The realistic review time per item
- Authority to reject the output
- Authority to stop the process
- Escalation path when something is wrong
- Accountability (who answers for the outcome)

## Test the loop

- Is the review realistic at the actual volume, or would it collapse under load?
- Does reviewing take as long as doing the task originally? If so, the AI did not save the work — it moved it.
- Can the reviewer actually detect the errors that matter (for example, a plausible but fabricated citation)?
- Is the reviewer expert enough to catch a subtle but consequential mistake?

## Keep human

Tasks that require professional legal judgment, exercise authority over students or others, or produce consequential external actions remain human decisions. AI may prepare or inform them; it does not own them.

## Output

Produce a short human-judgment map alongside any recommendation that includes AI, so the decision-maker sees precisely what people still do and whether that is feasible.
