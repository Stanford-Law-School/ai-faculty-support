# Stanford lens

Apply these considerations when a problem touches institutional mission, responsibility, evidence, data, agentic action, teaching, or equity. This is original faculty guidance, not binding Stanford policy, and it is not SLS-specific rules.

## Lenses

- **Mission and human purpose** — does the solution serve legitimate teaching, scholarship, or service, and keep human purpose central?
- **Responsibility** — who is accountable for outputs and actions?
- **Evidence** — are conclusions grounded in the actual problem and baseline, not impressions?
- **Data and privacy** — is the minimum necessary data used, with appropriate handling?
- **Agentic action** — if a system can act, are permissions least-privilege and consequential actions controlled?
- **Teaching and learning** — is students' learning and practice protected?
- **Equity and accessibility** — does the solution avoid excluding or disadvantaging anyone?

## Current-source rule

When current institutional or product facts matter, instruct the assistant to use live research and prioritize, in order:

1. Official Stanford sources
2. Primary legal or regulatory authority
3. Official product documentation
4. Primary research
5. Government or standards sources
6. Independent evaluations
7. Credible reporting

Record the research-checked date. Do not describe general Stanford University guidance as SLS-specific, and do not infer approval or prohibition from the absence of a public listing.

## Uncertain-status wording

"I did not locate a current public Stanford source establishing that exact status. That does not establish that the use is approved or prohibited. Confirmation is needed for the specific product, account, data, and workflow."

## Boundary

This Skill does not grant institutional approval, authorize data, or certify compliance. When those are needed, name them as authorized-review steps.
