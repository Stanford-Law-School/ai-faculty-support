# Root-cause analysis

Before matching a solution, test why the problem persists. Many problems attributed to "not having AI" are really process, information, or staffing problems that AI would not fix.

## Candidate root causes

Test each against the evidence:

- Process (unclear, redundant, or broken steps)
- Information (hard to find, scattered, outdated)
- Staffing (too few people, wrong roles)
- Training (people lack the knowledge or practice)
- Policy (rules unclear, missing, or misaligned)
- Access (people cannot reach what they need)
- Incentives (no reason to do it well or on time)
- Coordination (handoffs, dependencies, communication)
- Technology (genuinely missing capability)

## Method

- Ask "why" repeatedly until the answer is a cause, not another symptom.
- Separate the presenting symptom (slow, error-prone, overwhelming) from its cause.
- Check whether the same cause produces other problems too.

## Consequences for the recommendation

| If the root cause is... | Then the likely fix is... |
|---|---|
| Process | Redesign or remove steps first |
| Information | Better search, consolidation, or retrieval |
| Staffing | Role change; AI may only relocate work |
| Training | Instruction and practice |
| Policy | Clarify rules (route policy drafting to the Policy Builder) |
| Access | Accessibility and permissions fixes |
| Coordination | Handoff and communication changes |
| Technology | A tool may genuinely help — continue the analysis |

Only a genuine technology or capability gap makes AI a leading candidate; even then, weigh it against the non-AI options.
