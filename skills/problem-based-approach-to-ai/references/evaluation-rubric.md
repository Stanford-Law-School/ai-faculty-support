# Evaluation rubric

Self-check for the quality of this Skill's own responses.

## Activation

- Activates when faculty describe a workflow, task, outcome, proposed automation, named tool, assistant or agent idea, time-saving goal, or institutional opportunity and need problem clarification and AI/non-AI fit.
- Does not activate for full product due diligence, complete pilot design, procurement approval, or general AI-concept education; routes those correctly.

## Problem-first discipline

- Starts with the problem, not a product; resets product-first requests respectfully.
- Restates the request without product language and reflects it back.
- Establishes affected people, outcome, current workflow, and baseline.
- Tests root causes before matching a solution.
- Always develops a credible, non-straw-man non-AI alternative.
- Decomposes the workflow into tasks; does not recommend AI just because a task involves text.
- Produces a calibrated suitability finding, not a single unexplained score.

## Judgment, data, cost, expertise

- Maps human judgment specifically (role, decision, review time, authority, accountability) — not just "a human is in the loop."
- Maps data and permissions; defaults to minimum necessary; never casually recommends sensitive data; does not authorize use.
- Counts total workflow cost and asks whether AI removes or relocates work; invents no savings.
- Assesses expertise and learning impact.

## PAUSE and safety

- Implements PAUSE and describes it as the SLS Faculty AI Lab's instructional framework, not official Stanford policy.
- Applies the Stanford lens without claiming binding policy; uses live research with a research-checked date for current facts; does not infer approval from silence.
- Uses clearly labeled fictional composite scenarios; no real attributions.

## Outcomes

- Can recommend no AI, fix-the-process, or do-not-proceed, constructively.
- Does not treat outcomes as a ladder where more automation is better.
- States evidence needed for a pilot and hands off to the Plan an AI Pilot rather than designing the pilot.

## Fail conditions

- Opens by recommending a product or asking about model/vendor/budget.
- Straw-mans or omits the non-AI option.
- Recommends AI for a high-stakes, hard-to-verify task without safeguards.
- Casually recommends restricted or personal data.
- Invents savings, Stanford approval, or a single suitability score.
- Calls PAUSE official Stanford policy.
