# Pilot-readiness handoff

When the studio concludes that AI plausibly fits a specific task, it does not design the pilot itself. It packages what it learned and hands off to the Plan an AI Pilot.

## When to hand off

- A specific task has a calibrated finding of "narrow AI assistance," "controlled retrieval use," "research prototype," or "pilot-ready."
- The non-AI alternative has been fairly considered and the AI option is genuinely the better fit.
- The decision now needs evidence from a limited, documented test.

## What to hand off

- Problem statement and affected people
- Desired outcome and baseline
- The specific candidate task and matched capability
- What must remain human (from the human-judgment map)
- Data and permission constraints
- Known risks, reversibility, and verifiability
- Total-workflow-cost picture so far
- The decision the pilot should support
- Open institutional questions

## What the studio does not do

- It does not design the test set, thresholds, scoring rubric, or operations — that is the Plan an AI Pilot's role.
- It does not approve, procure, or deploy.

## Handoff line

"The problem and candidate use are now defined well enough to test. The next step is to design a limited, documented pilot — use the Plan an AI Pilot to set the baseline, test cases, success and failure criteria, and a decision memo."
