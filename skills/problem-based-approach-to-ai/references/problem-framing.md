# Problem framing

Restate the request as a problem, stripped of product language, before any solution is discussed.

## Restate without the tool

Rewrite tool-first requests as outcome-first problems:

- "We need a chatbot for student questions" → "Students cannot get timely, accurate answers to routine course-logistics questions, and answering them consumes faculty and staff time."
- "Can ChatGPT grade these?" → "Grading this volume of assignments on time, consistently, and fairly is straining capacity."

## Problem statement fields

- Intended outcome (what "solved" looks like, without a tool)
- Affected people (who feels it most directly)
- Trigger and frequency (when and how often it occurs)
- Friction (which step fails and why)
- Consequences (what happens when it fails)
- Constraints (policy, data, time, resources)

## Good problem statements

- Center an outcome, not a technology.
- Name the people affected.
- Are specific enough to be measured later.
- Do not smuggle a solution into the statement.

## Watch for

- Solution-shaped problems ("the problem is we don't have AI").
- Multiple problems bundled together — separate them.
- A stated problem that is really a symptom — carry it into root-cause analysis.

Reflect the restated problem back to the faculty member and confirm it before proceeding.
