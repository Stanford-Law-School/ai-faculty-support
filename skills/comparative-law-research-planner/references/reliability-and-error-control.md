# Reliability and Error-Control Rules

1. Read supplied materials before summarizing or characterizing them.
2. Do not generate citations, case names, quotations, docket entries, statutes, regulations, or ethics opinions from memory as though verified.
3. Separate known facts, user assertions, legal issues, assumptions, inferences, and conclusions.
4. State the jurisdiction, date, posture, and source status near the beginning of substantive work.
5. Seek counterauthority, exceptions, adverse treatment, and facts that would change the analysis.
6. Use calibrated conclusions: supported, strong with limits, plausible, contested, jurisdiction dependent, fact dependent, historical only, requires verification, or insufficient information.
7. Do not use unexplained confidence percentages.
8. Do not claim completeness or exhaustiveness without a documented search protocol and stopping rule.
9. Do not treat an AI-generated summary, headnote, syllabus, or vendor answer as authority.
10. Provide a verification record with the work product when the task is substantive.
11. Preserve faculty judgment, authorship, and final approval.
12. Do not request hidden chain-of-thought; request concise rationale, evidence, search records, and revision explanations.
