# SLS Research Database Guide

Use this local guide to identify likely Stanford Law School research databases and access links. It is based on the database information supplied by the Robert Crown Law Library and the accompanying database URL mapping workbook. Treat access, licensing, product features, and coverage as changeable. Verify current availability before making a definitive claim.

## Selection principles

- Start with the research question and source type, not a favorite platform.
- Use authoritative primary law and a citator when current legal validity matters.
- Use several databases when coverage, indexing, historical depth, or disciplinary scope differs.
- Prefer SLS access links over vendor marketing pages for actual research access.
- Do not treat an AI-generated answer as a substitute for database research or source reading.
- Contact `library@law.stanford.edu` when access, coverage, or database choice is uncertain.

## Frequently useful starting points

- **Lexis for Law Schools** - broad U.S. primary and secondary law, Shepard's, news, practical materials, and selected AI features.
- **Westlaw** - broad U.S. primary and secondary law, KeyCite, Key Numbers, dockets, analytics, and practical materials.
- **Bloomberg Law** - primary and secondary law, strong federal docket research, business intelligence, news, and analytics.
- **HeinOnline** - law journals, historical legal materials, scanned primary sources, treaties, session laws, and specialized collections.
- **SSRN / Legal Scholarship Network** - working papers, early scholarship, calls for papers, and scholarly announcements.
- **ProQuest Congressional / Legislative Insight / Regulatory Insight** - legislative and regulatory history.
- **Foreign Law Guide / Index to Foreign Legal Periodicals / Oxford international-law resources** - foreign, comparative, and international research.
- **PolicyMap, Gallup Analytics, TRACfed, BankruptcyData, Lex Machina, and other specialist data tools** - empirical and institutional research when the dataset fits the question.
