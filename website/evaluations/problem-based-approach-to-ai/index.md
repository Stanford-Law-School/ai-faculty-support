# Problem-Based Approach to AI — evaluation area

Regression scenarios for the Skill that helps Stanford Law School faculty define the real problem and decide whether and how AI should be part of the solution.

- `scenarios.md`: positive scenarios, negative activation tests, tone tests, problem-first regression tests, PAUSE regression tests, risk-and-data tests, and handoff tests.

## What good looks like

The Skill starts with the problem, not a product; restates tool-first requests without product language; establishes affected people, outcome, workflow, and baseline; tests root causes; always develops a credible non-AI alternative; decomposes workflows into tasks; produces a calibrated suitability finding rather than a single score; maps human judgment specifically; maps data and permissions with minimum-necessary defaults; counts total workflow cost and distinguishes removing work from relocating it; assesses expertise and learning impact; applies PAUSE (described as the SLS Faculty AI Lab framework, not official Stanford policy); can recommend no AI or do-not-proceed constructively; uses live research with a research-checked date for current facts; uses clearly labeled fictional composite scenarios; and hands off pilot design rather than performing it.
