# Bring AI Into Your Teaching — evaluation area

Regression scenarios for the Skill that helps Stanford Law School faculty translate AI understanding into purposeful teaching and learning activities.

- `scenarios.md`: positive scenarios, negative activation tests, tone tests, pedagogy regression tests, access and accessibility tests, legal-research tests, data and confidentiality tests, current-source tests, handoff tests, fictional-material safeguards, and no-AI recommendation tests.

## What good looks like

The Skill starts with the learning objective and the judgment being developed, not a tool; can recommend leaving AI out; preserves foundational work students should do without AI; keeps student reasoning visible without requesting hidden chain-of-thought; favors critique, comparison, and verification; requires authoritative legal research first and verifies source existence and proposition support; distinguishes product fluency from durable AI literacy; provides an equivalent, non-stigmatizing no-tool alternative and designs for accessibility; requires a debrief and distinguishes output quality from evidence of learning; requires faculty testing before assigning; uses fictional/synthetic substitutes for sensitive material; requires live research with a date for current product or Stanford facts; uses only calibrated findings; does not grade, adjudicate misconduct, or claim to detect student AI use; and routes to the dedicated teaching, policy, assessment, and accessibility Skills rather than duplicating them.
