# Test AI Governance — evaluation area

Regression scenarios for the Skill that helps Stanford Law School faculty reason about AI governance — accountability, decision rights, meaningful human judgment, permissions, evidence gates, transparency, correction, monitoring, incident response, stopping authority, and renewed review — through fictional-composite simulations and structured decision maps.

- `scenarios.md`: positive simulation scenarios, negative activation tests, tone tests, human-judgment regression tests, permission/least-privilege tests, incident/redress tests, current-Stanford-source tests, handoff tests, fictional-scenario safeguards, and planned-versus-implemented availability tests.

## What good looks like

The Skill treats governance as decision rights across the workflow; names an accountable purpose owner; separates access from authority and separates action levels; defaults to least privilege and read-only-first; requires confirmation before consequential external action; assesses meaningful human judgment and labels ceremonial oversight; maps the data lifecycle and minimizes; distinguishes evidence gates from authorization gates and never grants deployment authorization; includes transparency (not consent), contestability, correction, monitoring without indefinite retention, incident response, and explicit stop authority; triggers renewed review on material change; distinguishes vendor claims from technical controls and institutional authorization; requires live official-source research for current Stanford or product facts; uses clearly labeled fictional composite scenarios that identify no real person; uses only calibrated findings; and grants no authorization.
